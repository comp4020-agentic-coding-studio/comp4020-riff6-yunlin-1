# Policies and support

## Late work

Work submitted late loses 5% of the available mark per day, to a
maximum of five days, after which it is marked as not submitted. This
applies uniformly to all three assessments; there is no separate,
harsher rule for the final project. If a cut deserved more time, say so
in an extension request rather than in the defence you submit late.

## Extensions

Email the convenor before the due date, not after. A short, specific
reason is enough --- this course does not require supporting
documentation for extensions of five days or fewer. Extensions beyond
five days go through the standard university process.

## Use of generative AI

You may use generative AI tools to research, outline or get feedback on
a draft. You may not submit AI-generated prose, code or media as your
own work in any of the three assessments, and you may not submit an
AI-generated account of what you left out and why --- the course's whole
premise is that this is a judgement you make yourself, so a defence
written for you is a defence about nothing. If you used a tool anywhere
in your process, say how in a one-line note attached to your
submission; undisclosed use is treated as an academic integrity matter.

## Accessibility

Every seminar can be attended remotely on request, no explanation
needed, and slide decks are posted in advance of the lecture they
belong to, not after. If a set text, recording or studio material is not
accessible to you in the format it was provided, tell either teacher
directly --- this is treated as a course design gap to fix, not an
individual accommodation to argue for.

## Getting help

Marisol holds office hours weekly; Idris is reachable during and just
after each seminar, or by email between them --- see each person's page
under [People](/people/) for specifics. For anything that can't wait
until the next seminar or office hour, email --- the course does not
use a separate chat platform.
